# Transparency Guidelines for AI Content

A framework for honest, clear disclosure about AI use in content experiences.

## Why Transparency Matters

**Users deserve to know when they're interacting with AI.**

**Benefits of transparency:**
- **Builds trust** - Honesty creates credibility
- **Sets expectations** - Users know what AI can/can't do
- **Enables informed consent** - Users choose to engage
- **Legal compliance** - Many regulations require disclosure
- **Reduces harm** - Clear limitations prevent over-reliance

**Costs of opacity:**
- **Erodes trust** - Users feel deceived when they discover AI
- **Increases liability** - Hidden AI use creates legal risk
- **Damages brand** - Perceived as dishonest or manipulative
- **Disappoints users** - Unclear capabilities lead to frustration

## Core Principles

### 1. **Disclose AI Use**

**Users should know they're interacting with AI.**

**When to disclose:**

✅ **Always disclose:**
- Chatbots and conversational AI
- AI-generated content shown to users
- AI making decisions affecting users
- AI collecting or processing user data

⚠️ **Consider disclosing:**
- AI-assisted content (human-written with AI help)
- Behind-the-scenes AI (recommendations, ranking)
- AI in creative processes

❌ **May not need to disclose:**
- Basic automation (not AI)
- Standard algorithms
- Internal tools not affecting users

**Disclosure examples:**

```markdown
Explicit (Recommended):
"I'm an AI assistant powered by [Model Name]"
"This summary was generated by AI"
"AI-assisted content, reviewed by experts"

Visual Indicators:
🤖 AI icon or avatar
"AI" badge on messages
Different visual styling

Contextual:
In settings/about section
In terms of service
In privacy policy
```

---

### 2. **Explain Capabilities & Limitations**

**Users should understand what AI can and can't do.**

**What to explain:**

**Capabilities:**
```markdown
✅ Clear:
"I can help you:
• Answer questions about [specific topic]
• Guide you through [specific process]
• Provide information from [knowledge source]"

❌ Vague:
"I can help with anything!"
```

**Limitations:**
```markdown
✅ Honest:
"I can't:
• Provide medical, legal, or financial advice
• Access your personal data or make changes
• Know information after [training date]
• Understand images or attachments"

❌ Silent:
[Don't mention limitations, hope user doesn't notice]
```

**Accuracy:**
```markdown
✅ Transparent:
"I might make mistakes. Always verify important information."
"I'm powered by AI and may not always be accurate."
"For critical decisions, consult a human expert."

❌ Overconfident:
"I'm always accurate!"
[No mention of potential errors]
```

---

### 3. **Show How AI Works**

**Users should understand the AI's decision-making process.**

**Levels of explanation:**

**Basic (Minimum):**
```markdown
"I'm an AI trained on [type of data] to [purpose]"

Example:
"I'm an AI assistant trained on Fabric documentation 
to help answer questions about the platform."
```

**Intermediate:**
```markdown
"I use [technology] to [process].
I was trained on [data sources] and can [capabilities]."

Example:
"I use natural language processing to understand your questions
and search through Fabric documentation to find relevant answers.
I was trained on our help articles, user guides, and API docs."
```

**Advanced:**
```markdown
Include:
• Model architecture
• Training data sources
• How responses are generated
• Confidence scores
• Why this answer was chosen

Example:
"I found this answer by:
1. Understanding your question about pipeline scheduling
2. Searching documentation (85% relevance match)
3. Summarizing the top 3 results
4. Verifying against our knowledge base

Confidence: High (87%)"
```

**When to provide more detail:**
```markdown
More detail needed when:
- High-stakes decisions
- Unexpected/surprising results
- User questions how it works
- Regulatory requirements
- Building trust in new AI

Less detail needed when:
- Low-stakes interactions
- Well-understood AI use
- User-initiated experience
```

**Users should be able to opt in/out and control their data.**

**Essential controls:**

```markdown
✅ Must provide:
☐ Option to talk to human instead
☐ Way to report issues
☐ Ability to delete conversation history
☐ Control over data usage (opt-in/out of training)
☐ Explanation of how data is used

Consider providing:
☐ Adjust AI behavior (tone, verbosity)
☐ See AI's data sources
☐ Export conversation history
☐ Turn off AI features entirely
```

### 5. **Admit Uncertainty**

**AI should acknowledge when it doesn't know something.**

**Don't fake confidence:**

```markdown
❌ Bad (Confident but wrong):
User: "What's the maximum file size for uploads?"
AI: "The maximum file size is 500MB."
[Actual limit: 100MB]

✅ Good (Honest about uncertainty):
AI: "I'm not certain of the exact file size limit. 
Let me check the documentation."
[Retrieves accurate info]

Or: "I don't have that specific information. 
Check the documentation or contact support for the exact limit."
```

**Confidence indicators:**

```markdown
High confidence:
"According to the documentation, [fact]."

Medium confidence:
"Based on what I know, [info], but you should verify this."

Low confidence:
"I'm not certain, but you might want to check [source]."

No confidence:
"I don't know. For accurate information, please [alternative]."
```

**When to say "I don't know":**

```markdown
Say "I don't know" when:
- Information not in training data
- Question outside scope
- Confidence below threshold
- Request requires judgment
- Ambiguous or unclear query

Provide:
- Alternative resources
- Human contact option
- How to find answer
- Related information you do know
```

### 6. **Source Attribution**

**Show where information comes from.**

**Why it matters:**
- Users can verify
- Builds credibility
- Enables fact-checking
- Shows transparency
- Helps users learn more

**How to attribute:**

```markdown
Inline citations:
"Pipeline runs are limited to 24 hours [documentation link]."

Source panel:
[Response]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Sources:
• Pipeline Documentation
• FAQ: Common Limits
• Release Notes v2.3
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Confidence with source:
"According to the Fabric API guide (docs.fabric.com/api):
[quoted information]"

Multiple sources:
"Based on:
• Official documentation
• Community forum discussions
• Support team guidance
[Response synthesizing these sources]"
```

## Transparency Patterns

### Pattern 1: Explicit Bot Introduction

**First interaction:**

```markdown
👋 Hi! I'm Fabric AI Assistant

I'm an AI trained to help with:
• Questions about Fabric features
• Troubleshooting common issues
• Guidance through workflows

I might not always be right, so verify important information.
Need human help? Click "Talk to Support" anytime.

How can I help you today?
```

### Pattern 2: Confidence Indicator

**Show confidence visually:**

```markdown
User: "How do I create a pipeline?"

AI: [Response with step-by-step instructions]

Confidence: ████████░░ 80% (High)

This answer is based on current documentation.
[View sources] [Was this helpful? 👍 👎]
```

### Pattern 3: Human Handoff

**Clear transition:**

```markdown
AI: "This question is outside my area. Let me connect 
you with a human expert who can help better."

[Connecting you to support...]

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Support Agent (Sarah): Hi! I can help with that. 
I see you were asking about [topic]. Let me look into this...
```

### Pattern 4: Iterative Transparency

**Progressive disclosure:**

```markdown
Initial (brief):
🤖 AI Assistant

On hover/click:
"AI-powered help based on Fabric documentation.
Click for details."

Details page:
"About AI Assistant
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

• May not always be perfect - verify important info

Your privacy:
• Conversations stored 30 days (you can delete anytime)
• Not used for training without your consent
• No personal data shared

[Manage AI Settings]"
```

### Pattern 5: Disclaimer Integration

**Context-appropriate warnings:**

```markdown
Medical query:
⚠️ AI cannot provide medical advice
[AI attempts to help] → Redirects to qualified resources

Financial query:
ℹ️ Not financial advice - consult a professional
[AI provides general information only]

Legal query:
⚠️ This is not legal advice
[AI gives educational info, suggests lawyer]

Opinion query:
ℹ️ AI Opinion (Not fact-based)
[AI clarifies when offering subjective view]
```

## Legal & Regulatory Compliance

### Required by Law (Varies by Jurisdiction)

> This section summarizes public sources for content designers. It isn't legal advice. Confirm current requirements with your legal team.

**EU AI Act, Article 50 (transparency), enforceable since August 2, 2026:**

| Obligation | Who it applies to | What content has to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Chatbot and interactive AI disclosure** | Providers | Tell people they're interacting with an AI system, unless it's obvious from context |
| **Machine-readable marking of synthetic content** | Providers | Mark generated audio, image, video, and text so it's detectable as AI-generated (assistive editing is exempt; a separate runway for watermarking runs to December 2, 2026) |
| **Deepfake labeling** | Deployers | Disclose that content is artificially generated or manipulated; evidently artistic, satirical, or fictional work has lighter requirements |
| **AI-generated text on matters of public interest** | Deployers | Disclose that it's AI-generated, unless a person with editorial responsibility reviewed it |
| **Emotion recognition and biometric categorization** | Deployers | Inform the people exposed to it |

Disclosures must be made at the latest at the time of the first interaction and must be accessible. Penalties reach €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. The European Commission has published a voluntary icon set for labeling AI-generated content. ([Summary: Cloud Security Alliance research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-20260729/))

**What this means for content:** write the AI disclosure into the first screen or first message of every AI interaction, make it readable by screen readers, and make sure exports and downloads of AI-generated media carry machine-readable markers such as C2PA Content Credentials.

**EU AI Act (high-risk AI systems):**
- [ ] Transparent about AI use
- [ ] Human oversight mechanisms
- [ ] Technical documentation
- [ ] Risk management system
- [ ] Conformity assessments

Timelines for high-risk obligations were revised by the EU's Digital Omnibus package, so check the current dates for your system's category.

**GDPR (Data Protection):**
- [ ] Clear consent for data processing
- [ ] Explanation of automated decisions
- [ ] Right to human review
- [ ] Data portability
- [ ] Right to deletion

**US State Laws (California, Colorado, etc.):**
- [ ] Disclosure of AI decision-making
- [ ] Opt-out mechanisms
- [ ] Data usage transparency
- [ ] Non-discrimination protections

**Industry-Specific:**
- **Financial:** FCRA (Fair Credit Reporting Act)
- **Healthcare:** HIPAA compliance for PHI
- **Education:** FERPA for student data
- **Children:** COPPA for under-13 users

## Testing Transparency

### User Research Questions

**Test comprehension:**
```markdown
After showing disclosure:

1. "In your own words, what is this?"
   → Do they understand it's AI?

2. "What can this system do?"
   → Do they understand capabilities?

3. "What can't it do?"
   → Do they understand limitations?

4. "How much do you trust this?"
   → Does transparency build trust?

5. "Would you use this?"
   → Does disclosure affect adoption?
```

**Test adequacy:**
```markdown
1. "Do you have enough information to decide?"
   → Is disclosure sufficient?

2. "What else would you want to know?"
   → What's missing?

3. "Is this too much/little information?"
   → Right level of detail?

4. "Where would you look for more details?"
   → Is navigation clear?
```

### A/B Testing Disclosures

**Test different approaches:**

```markdown
Variant A: Explicit upfront
"I'm an AI assistant..."

Variant B: Contextual
AI badge, details on click

Variant C: Progressive
Disclose after first interaction

Measure:
• User trust (survey)
• Usage rate
• Satisfaction
• Completion rate
• Feedback quality
```

## Common Mistakes

### ❌ DON'T:

**Hide AI in fine print**
```markdown
❌ Bad:
Page 47 of terms: "May use automated systems"

✅ Good:
Clear, prominent disclosure on first use
```

**Pretend to be human**
```markdown
❌ Bad:
"Hi, I'm Sarah! How can I help?"
[Actually AI with no disclosure]

✅ Good:
"Hi! I'm an AI assistant. How can I help?"
```

**Over-promise capabilities**
```markdown
❌ Bad:
"I can answer any question perfectly!"

✅ Good:
"I can help with [specific topics], but I might make mistakes."
```

**Bury limitations**
```markdown
❌ Bad:
[No mention of what AI can't do]

✅ Good:
Clear list of capabilities AND limitations
```

**Make disclosure hard to find**
```markdown
❌ Bad:
[About link] → [AI info link] → [Disclosure buried in text]

✅ Good:
Prominent disclosure, easy to access details
```

**Use jargon**
```markdown
❌ Bad:
"Powered by a fine-tuned large language model with RAG and vector embeddings"

✅ Good:
"AI assistant trained on our documentation"
```

### ✅ DO:

**Be clear and direct**
State AI use plainly

**Front-load disclosure**
Tell users immediately

**Explain in plain language**
No technical jargon needed

**Highlight limitations**
Be honest about what AI can't do

**Provide easy controls**
Simple opt-out and data management

**Test with real users**
Ensure disclosure is understood

## How leading companies do it

**IBM: one label, everywhere, leading to an explanation.** Carbon's AI label marks AI content from a single word to a full page. Selecting it opens a popover with an overview, supporting details, artifacts and resources, and actions. The label is never decorative, never doubles as a button, and is never disabled. ([AI label](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/ai-label/usage/))

**Meta: word labels to match what you know.** Meta's "Made with AI" label was applied to real photos with minor AI edits; after objections from photographers, Meta renamed it "AI info," explaining that generative AI *may* have been used. Meta also detects C2PA and IPTC metadata and asks people to self-disclose realistic AI video and audio. ([Report](https://jang.com.pk/en/15206-meta-replaces-made-with-ai-label-with-ai-info-amid-user-criticism-news), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/meta-plans-to-ramp-up-labeling-of-ai-generated-images-across-its-platforms-160234038.html))

**Adobe: provenance that travels with the content.** Content Credentials record who made content and how, including AI use; Firefly attaches them automatically, and a Cr icon reveals them. ([Content Credentials](https://helpx.adobe.com/in/creative-cloud/apps/adobe-content-authenticity/content-credentials/overview.html))

**Microsoft: disclose, remind, cite.** Microsoft 365 Copilot tells people they're interacting with AI, reminds them AI-generated content may contain errors, and cites sources where possible. Microsoft's broader guidelines ask products to make clear why the system did what it did (G11). ([Copilot transparency note](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-transparency-note))

**Amazon: publish limitations.** AWS AI Service Cards state intended use cases and limitations for each AI service in a standard format. ## Key Takeaways

1. **Always disclose AI use** - Users have a right to know
2. **Be honest about limitations** - Don't overpromise
3. **Provide user control** - Let users opt out and manage data
4. **Make it understandable** - Plain language, not jargon
5. **Test comprehension** - Ensure users actually understand
6. **Stay compliant** - Know your legal requirements
7. **Iterate** - Transparency practices evolve with AI

---

**Remember:** Transparency is not just about legal compliance. It's about respecting users and building trust. Honest disclosure about AI use, capabilities, and limitations creates better relationships and better products.
